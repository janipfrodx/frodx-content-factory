# Content-Type Rules

## Blog column

Goal: preserve Igor's editorial voice, argument, and narrative flow.

Transformation intensity: high.

Rules:

- Keep the text essayistic, not listicle-like.
- Preserve the personal or anecdotal opening if present.
- If the source opening is generic, make it more concrete without inventing facts.
- Keep the provocative thesis visible.
- Preserve concrete examples, numbers, and cases.
- Do not over-compress. A column should have room to develop.
- Avoid generic introductions and formal conclusions.
- Use strong but natural headings if headings exist in the source.
- Preserve first person when present.
- Translate or recreate humor and self-irony only if it works naturally in the target language.
- Reorder sentences or paragraphs if the target language needs a more natural editorial flow.

The final text should read like an original column by a senior practitioner, not a translated article. Match Igor's measured rhythm (see voice-and-style.md): average ~11 words, ~48 % of sentences under 8 words - NOT 15–18; max two one-liners. If `igor-column-writer` is installed, borrow its rhythm calibration and golden examples; this skill handles the language transfer, that one defines the column voice.

## Newsletter

Goal: make the copy feel like a personal business email from Igor/FrodX to existing customers, subscribers, or followers.

Transformation intensity: high.

Rules:

- Keep it conversational, experienced, and lightly provocative.
- Use a natural opening, often based on a concrete observation or short anecdote.
- Keep the CTA soft: invite reading, listening, watching, replying, or a short conversation.
- Avoid sounding like a campaign blast.
- Preserve directness, but do not make the tone aggressive.
- Make transitions smooth when several pieces of content are promoted.
- Avoid excessive formatting unless the source uses it.
- Do not turn the newsletter into a press release or formal announcement.

## Outbound email

Goal: produce a short, direct prospecting email in Igor's style.

Transformation intensity: controlled.

Rules:

- Use first person.
- Use polite business address in Slovenian/Croatian contexts.
- Keep it concise: usually 4-5 sentences.
- Keep sentences short and native; do not pad to a target length. Igor's editorial rhythm runs short (~11 words average) - naturalness comes first.
- Structure: observation -> challenge -> solution/differentiator -> question.
- Do not start with an introduction to FrodX.
- Do not use a meeting CTA in the first email unless explicitly requested.
- End with a relevant question, not a generic booking request.
- Do not use generic personalization.
- Do not mention industries outside the approved terminology list.
- For finance, mention DORA compliance only when supported by the context.

## LinkedIn post

Goal: make the idea clear, sharp, and native to the platform without turning it into cliche thought leadership.

Transformation intensity: high.

Rules:

- Start with a strong observation or tension.
- Use compact paragraphs.
- Avoid excessive emojis, hashtags, and formulaic line breaks unless requested.
- Preserve Igor's practical and slightly provocative voice.
- Make the point useful without sounding like a motivational post.
- Do not overuse bullets. Use them only when they improve readability.
- Keep the final line as a thought, challenge, or invitation, not a generic engagement bait question.

## Web page

Goal: produce clear, structured, credible commercial copy.

Transformation intensity: medium.

Rules:

- Be clearer and more structured than a newsletter or column.
- Use less personal phrasing unless the source is explicitly personal.
- Avoid generic SaaS or agency language.
- Make value propositions concrete.
- Keep claims tied to what the source supports.
- Use headings and sections naturally.
- Make CTAs precise and context-appropriate.
- Improve information architecture if the source is unclear, but do not invent new offers.

## Sales offer or proposal

Goal: produce precise, credible, commercially useful copy.

Transformation intensity: low.

Rules:

- Be specific about scope, outcomes, assumptions, and limits.
- Do not inflate promises.
- Preserve all numbers, deliverables, timelines, dependencies, pricing, validity terms, assumptions, and exclusions.
- Use business language that decision-makers can trust.
- Avoid storytelling unless the section requires positioning or rationale.
- Improve clarity where the source is vague, but do not add unsupported commitments.
- Do not restructure so aggressively that contractual meaning could change.

## Generic business text

Goal: produce natural, senior-level business copy in the target language.

Transformation intensity: medium by default.

Rules:

- Infer the likely audience and purpose from the source.
- Preserve the core message and intent.
- Rewrite for native rhythm and clarity.
- Remove visible translation artifacts.
- Keep FrodX terminology and Igor's tone where relevant.
